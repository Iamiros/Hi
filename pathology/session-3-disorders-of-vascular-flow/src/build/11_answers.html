Q1 — CAlveolar macrophages that have phagocytosed extravasated red cells. Chronic left heart failure raises left atrial and hence pulmonary capillary hydrostatic pressure. The over-distended, 0.5 µm-thick septal capillaries rupture in small numbers, spilling red cells into the alveoli. Alveolar macrophages phagocytose them; haemoglobin is degraded, but the liberated iron cannot be excreted and is stored as haemosiderin — the coarse golden-brown, Prussian-blue-positive granules. These are heart failure cells (siderophages), and they are why the sputum is rust-coloured. D Surfactant is not pigmented and does not stain with Prussian blue. E Myeloperoxidase produces no visible pigment and the cells described are mononuclear, not neutrophils. A Anthracotic carbon pigment is black and Prussian-blue negative, because it contains no iron — this is the key discriminator. B Keratin is not granular, not brown, and not iron-containing. See §1.5.ماکروفاژهای آلوئولی که گلبول‌های قرمز خارج‌شده را بلعیده‌اند. نارسایی مزمن قلب چپ فشار دهلیز چپ و در نتیجه فشار هیدروستاتیک مویرگ ریوی را بالا می‌برد. مویرگ‌های سپتوم با ضخامت نیم میکرومتر که بیش از حد متسع شده‌اند، تعداد کمی پاره می‌شوند و گلبول قرمز به آلوئول می‌ریزد. ماکروفاژهای آلوئولی آن‌ها را می‌بلعند؛ هموگلوبین تجزیه می‌شود اما آهن آزادشده قابل دفع نیست و به‌صورت هموسیدرین ذخیره می‌گردد — همان دانه‌های درشت قهوه‌ای–طلایی و مثبت با رنگ پروس. این‌ها سلول‌های نارسایی قلبی هستند و دلیل زنگاری بودن خلط. د سورفکتانت رنگدانه ندارد و با پروس رنگ نمی‌گیرد. ه میلوپراکسیداز رنگدانهٔ قابل‌رؤیت تولید نمی‌کند و سلول‌های توصیف‌شده تک‌هسته‌ای‌اند نه نوتروفیل. الف رنگدانهٔ کربنی آنتراکوتیک سیاه و از نظر پروس منفی است، چون آهن ندارد — تمایزدهندهٔ کلیدی همین است. ب کراتین نه دانه‌دار است، نه قهوه‌ای، و نه حاوی آهن. بخش ۱.۵.
Q2 — CCentrilobular (zone 3). Two independent facts converge. First, blood leaves the lobule through the central vein, so back-pressure from a failing right heart is transmitted to the central vein and perivenular sinusoids before anywhere else. Second, blood enters at the portal tracts and gives up oxygen as it travels inward, so zone 3 sees the lowest pO₂ and is the first to die when flow slows. Congestion and hypoxic necrosis therefore coincide in zone 3, producing the dark red foci; the surviving, often fatty periportal hepatocytes give the pale tan background. B Zone 1 receives oxygenated blood first and is the protected zone. D Zone 2 is intermediate, not extreme. E Portal tract fibrosis is the pattern of biliary disease, not cardiac congestion. A The lesion is strictly zonal — that is precisely what creates the regular nutmeg mottling. The periorbital distribution and the worsening on waking reflect the extreme looseness of periorbital tissue and the absence of gravity dependence. B Rising interstitial pressure opposes edema — it is the natural brake. C Would cause localised, non-pitting, protein-rich swelling, not generalised periorbital edema. Q4 — AProtein-rich fluid that has provoked interstitial fibrosis. Axillary node clearance severs the lymphatic channels that normally return escaped plasma protein to the blood. Protein accumulates in the interstitium, raising interstitial oncotic pressure and drawing out still more water — a self-perpetuating loop. Protein-rich fluid is viscous and displaces poorly under a finger; over years it also stimulates collagen deposition, producing brawny, woody induration. Both effects make the swelling non-pitting. B Low specific gravity describes a transudate, which pits readily. C A neutrophil-rich fluid would be an inflammatory exudate — there is no infection here. D Anatomical location does not determine pitting. E Light's criteria classify effusions; they say nothing about pitting and would in any case describe a transudate. Q5 — ECongestive heart failure. Every number points to a transudate: fluid/serum protein 0.25 (<0.5), fluid/serum LDH 0.32 (<0.6), specific gravity 1.008 (<1.012), few cells, and no clotting because fibrinogen has not escaped. A transudate means the vessel wall is intact and the problem is a Starling-force imbalance — so look outside the pleura, at the heart, liver or kidney. Bilateral effusions strongly favour a systemic cause. B, C, D, E all produce exudates (increased permeability from inflammation or tumour), with protein >3 g/dL, high LDH, high cellularity, turbid fluid and clotting on standing. A pulmonary embolus with infarction also typically gives a unilateral, often blood-stained exudate. Q8 — DFactor VIII deficiency (haemophilia A). The pattern is textbook for a secondary hemostasis defect: deep bleeding into a joint (haemarthrosis), and delayed rebleeding hours after initial haemostasis was achieved. The explanation is mechanistic — the platelet plug forms normally (hence normal platelet count and bleeding time, and initial haemostasis) but is never reinforced by fibrin, so it disintegrates hours later. Factor VIII is in the intrinsic pathway, so PTT is prolonged and PT normal. E von Willebrand disease impairs platelet adhesion, so it causes mucocutaneous bleeding with a prolonged bleeding time — both absent here (though PTT can be prolonged because vWF carries factor VIII). A Factor VII is extrinsic, so it would prolong PT, not PTT. B Glanzmann thrombasthenia is a platelet aggregation defect — mucocutaneous bleeding, prolonged bleeding time, normal PTT. C Scurvy causes perifollicular haemorrhage and bleeding gums with normal coagulation studies. Q10 — EClass II haemorrhage (15–30%), and the normal haemoglobin is uninformative. Tachycardia with a narrowed pulse pressure (118/96 = 22 mmHg) is the signature of class II: sympathetic vasoconstriction raises diastolic pressure and maintains systolic pressure, so blood pressure looks deceptively normal. In acute haemorrhage, whole blood is lost — red cells and plasma in the same proportion — so the concentration of haemoglobin is initially unchanged; it falls only over hours as interstitial fluid shifts in to refill the vascular space. A Class I loss produces essentially no signs; he has marked tachycardia and peripheral shutdown. B This is the classic trap and is factually wrong, for the reason just given. C Class IV would show profound hypotension and obtundation. D A narrowed pulse pressure indicates vasoconstriction; septic shock classically gives a widened pulse pressure with warm peripheries.
